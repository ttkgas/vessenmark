Government of the Principality

The Principality of Vessenmark is governed under a written constitution adopted by its citizens in 2020. The head of state is Prince Aurel Dunmoreth, who founded Vessenmark in 2019. The seat of government is Harrowell, the capital, where the council holds its annual in-person session.

The Constitution

The Constitution of Vessenmark is a short document of twenty-two articles. It defines citizenship, sets out the duties of the Prince and the council, and lists the rights of citizens to vote, to stand for council and to read every decision made in their name. One of its opening articles states plainly that the laws of the country in which Harrowell lies apply in full to the Principality's land and to everyone on it. Vessenmark's own rules, called ordinances, govern only its internal affairs, such as the register, the treasury and the care of the meadow.

The Prince

Prince Aurel Dunmoreth serves as head of state. He represents Vessenmark in correspondence with other micronations, presides over Founding Day and signs ordinances once the council has passed them. The Prince chairs council meetings but votes only to break a tie. Under the Constitution, the Prince may name a successor, who must then be confirmed by a vote of the citizens.

The council

Day-to-day business is handled by a council of five members, elected by the citizens for two-year terms:

Chancellor
Leads the council's work between meetings and prepares its agenda.
Keeper of the Register
Maintains the list of citizens and handles new applications.
Treasurer
Manages the small fund that pays for upkeep of the cabin and the annual gathering.
Steward of Harrowell
Looks after the land, the cabin and the tree-planting program.
Councillor for Correspondence
Edits the quarterly bulletin and answers letters from citizens and other micronations.

How decisions are made

The council meets online each quarter and in person at Harrowell once a year. Ordinances pass by a simple majority of the council. Amending the Constitution requires four of the five councillors and a majority of citizens voting. Minutes of every meeting are published for citizens. Vessenmark remains a self-declared micronation without recognition from any UN member state.
